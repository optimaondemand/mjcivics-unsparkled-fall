Setting: the Comparative Government Gallery. Time: ~30 minutes. Category: Synchronous Homework & Activities.

This activity works whether you attend a live VR class session or complete it solo; the steps are identical either way. Enter the Comparative Government Gallery, set up with two facing walls: one labeled "U.S. Constitutional Republic," the other "Soviet Communist System." A posted placard on each wall summarizes that system's courts, press, and economy.

  1. At the U.S. wall, leave a note naming one specific institution from this course (a court case, a constitutional clause, an amendment) that acts as a check against the kind of abuse this week's readings describe.
  2. At the Soviet wall, leave a note naming the specific absence that allowed that abuse to happen: no independent press, no independent courts, or both.
  3. Stand at the center point between the two walls and use the whiteboard to draw one arrow connecting your two notes, labeling exactly what function the missing Soviet institution would have needed to serve.
  4. Record a short walkthrough of yourself narrating your full comparison and stating, in your own words, the single most important lesson you are taking from this course's study of American constitutional government.

Submit: the recording of your walkthrough, uploaded to the Canvas assignment. If completed live, your facilitator may also ask you to briefly share your comparison aloud.

Facilitator note: This is the module's featured graded VR activity (Synchronous Homework & Activities, 10% of course grade) and Quarter 2's closing reflection. Encourage specific, textually grounded comparisons rather than general statements that "America is better"; the goal is precise institutional reasoning, not slogans.
