Setting: the United Nations Security Council Chamber. Time: ~30 minutes. Category: Synchronous Homework & Activities.

This activity works whether you attend a live VR class session or complete it solo; the steps are identical either way. Enter the recreated Security Council Chamber. You will find the horseshoe table with member-state placards and a posted placard summarizing the 1990 Gulf crisis resolution process.

  1. Sit at a permanent member's seat (identified on the placard) and leave a note explaining what the veto power at that seat means for how the Council can act.
  2. Move to a NATO member's seat and leave a second note explaining how NATO's mutual-defense promise differs from the UN Security Council's collective-action process.
  3. Stand at the podium and use the whiteboard to sketch a simple flowchart: crisis occurs, UN resolution sought, coalition built, action taken, labeling where NATO could fit into that process if invoked.
  4. Record a short walkthrough of yourself narrating: which organization, the UN or NATO, do you think gives the United States more independent freedom of action, and which gives it more legitimacy in the eyes of other nations? Defend your answer in under two minutes.

Submit: the recording of your walkthrough, uploaded to the Canvas assignment. If completed live, your facilitator may also ask you to briefly share your answer aloud.

Facilitator note: This is the module's featured graded VR activity (Synchronous Homework & Activities, 10% of course grade). Students often conflate the UN and NATO; steer them back to the distinction between a near-universal collective-security forum (UN) and a specific mutual-defense alliance (NATO) if this surfaces.
